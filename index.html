02 — Why It Matters WHY THIS MATTERS

Most founder mistakes are money mistakes in disguise.

Hiring, pricing, fundraising, growth — they all look like business decisions. They're really money decisions. A CFO's job is to make those decisions clearly.

01 / Spend with confidence

Know what each dollar is buying

Every dollar you spend is a dollar you can't spend on something else. We help you see what each one is actually buying you — and whether it's worth it.

02 / Know how long your cash lasts

You probably have less time than you think

Most founders overestimate their cash by two to four months. We show you exactly how many months you really have, and what changes when you adjust spending.

03 / Know if you're actually making money

Growing fast isn't the same as making money

If you lose money on every customer, growth makes things worse, not better. We help you see the real picture per customer.

04 / Tell a story investors believe

Investors don't fund vibes

They fund founders who can explain their numbers simply and clearly. The same business, with and without that story, raises at very different prices.

05 / Charge the right price

A small price change beats a lot of hard work

Raising your price by just 1% often does more for your profit than working 10% harder. We help you find where you're leaving money on the table.

06 / Be ready when an investor says yes

When an offer comes, you have days, not months

Clean numbers and a clear plan are the difference between a great deal and a bad one. We get you ready before the moment arrives.

03 — The Cost of Waiting WHAT IT COSTS TO PUT THIS OFF

Putting this off is more expensive than you think.

Four things we see again and again in founders who waited a year too long. None of them are dramatic — and all of them are avoidable.

/ I

Raising less than
your company is worth

When you raise without a clear financial story, investors offer you less. We've seen founders lose 30–55% of their company's value just because the numbers weren't ready.

/ II

Hiring people you can't
actually afford

It's easy to hire based on what's coming in. What matters is what's left after costs. Hiring too fast usually ends in painful layoffs six months later.

/ III

Big customers who
quietly lose you money

Some of your biggest customers cost more to serve than they pay you. You wouldn't believe how often. A clear look at the numbers shows this in the first week.

/ IV

Signing a deal that quietly
takes your company from you

Investor contracts have small print that matters a lot. The wrong clause can mean walking away with far less than you expected, even after a great exit. Knowing this before you sign is the difference between winning and just selling.
